## A landmark, after the storm

Kingsbury Place is one of St. Louis's historic private streets, founded in 1902. On May 16, 2025, a tornado cut through the neighborhood, and the clay tile roof on this house, laid more than a century ago, took the blow. The owners wanted a new roof that still looked like the one the house was built with. Ours was the first full re-roof finished on the street after the storm, completed in October 2025.

## The house

William H. Gruen designed the house in 1908 in the Italian Renaissance manner: deep red brick banded in limestone, round arches carried across the first two stories, and a low hipped roof of clay tile settled over it all. Behind it stands the carriage house of 1909, roofed to match. The property sits within the Kingsbury Place City Landmark district and a National Register historic district, so every choice on the roof had to answer to the original design. The work took in the main house, the carriage house, the pool pavilion, the sunroom, and every bay and porch roof between them.

## A tile that no longer exists

The original roof was [Mound City tile](/blog/mound-city-roofing-tile-history), made here in St. Louis by a company that closed its doors in the 1920s. No one has made it since. What the owners loved most were its ornaments: the terminals that crown each meeting of the hips, and the hip starters that finish the eaves. They are small pieces, but they give the roof its character, and once they are lost, they cannot be replaced.

## Keeping what cannot be remade

Tear-off was slow on purpose. We removed the old roof by hand, handled every Mound City terminal and hip starter as delicately as we could, and protected them on the ground until they went back up. They were made for a different tile, so we cut and angled each piece to seat properly on the new roof. For the field, we chose [Ludowici](/materials/ludowici) Spanish 13, blending Summer Rose with Historic Clay Red. A single color would have looked new. Blending two gives the roof the warm, uneven color of tile that has weathered for a century, which helps the salvaged pieces sit naturally among the new.

Every piece of metal on the roof is [copper](/materials/copper): the valleys, the chimney flashing and counterflashing, the wall and sunroom flashings, the vent jacks, the gutters, and the downspouts, all bent and fit by hand on site. Copper will outlast the tile, turning a deep brown and, over decades, green. Under the tile we installed Titanium PSU 30, a high-temperature self-adhered underlayment. No one will ever see it, which is exactly why we don't cut corners on it.

## A custom copper awning

Over the side entry, we built a copper awning by hand, shaped to the arch of the masonry opening it shelters.
