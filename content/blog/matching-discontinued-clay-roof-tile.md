A storm takes out three hundred tiles on a 1929 home. The homeowner calls the manufacturer to order replacements and learns the profile was discontinued in 1962, the molds are gone, and the company that made them no longer exists. Three roofers come out and all three say the same thing: the whole roof has to come off, because the tile cannot be matched.

That is usually not true. Matching a discontinued clay tile is one of the things we do that almost no one else does, and it is one of the reasons this company exists.

## Why your tile is discontinued in the first place

Clay tile lasts a very long time, often longer than the company that made it. The American roofing landscape is full of beautiful tile from manufacturers that merged, closed, or simply retired a profile decades ago. Heinz, Mineral Wells, and dozens of regional makers produced tile that is still on roofs and will be for another fifty years, long after the molds were scrapped.

So a discontinued tile is not a sign of a failing roof. It is usually a sign of a good one. The clay is doing its job. You just need a few hundred more of it.

## What is actually possible

More than most contractors will tell you. There are several real paths to matching a discontinued profile, and the right one depends on the tile.

**Salvage.** There is a national market for reclaimed historic tile. A profile discontinued here may exist by the pallet on a building being demolished three states away. Sourcing salvage is detective work, and it is work most roofers will not do, but for many older profiles it is the cleanest match possible: the exact tile, aged the same way yours has.

**Remanufacture.** This is the one people do not know exists. Ludowici, the oldest and most renowned clay tile manufacturer in America, can remake molds dating back to their founding, and can match discontinued molds such as Heinz tile or Mineral Wells tile. That means a tile no one has produced in sixty years can be made new, in the right profile, to finish your roof in the original character. Not every profile can be remanufactured, but far more can than the average contractor realizes.

**Blending a close match.** When an exact match is genuinely unavailable, a skilled installer can blend a current profile into the existing field in a way the eye accepts, often by reserving the original tile for the most visible elevations and placing the new tile where it reads as the same roof.

**Relocating the original tile.** Sometimes the answer is to pull original tile from a hidden elevation, a rear slope or a section being altered anyway, use it to repair the prominent faces, and put the new or close-match tile where no one looks up at it.

## What is not possible, honestly

We will tell you the limits too, because pretending they do not exist is how clients get burned.

A tile that has aged sixty years in the Texas sun has a patina that brand-new clay does not. Even a perfect profile match in fresh tile will read slightly different until it weathers in, which it will, over a few seasons. A good installer plans the layout around this. A careless one scatters new tile across the front of the house and leaves you staring at it.

And some profiles genuinely cannot be matched, exactly or closely. When that is the case, we say so, and the conversation becomes an honest one about a full re-roof rather than a false promise about a match that does not exist.

## How to not make it worse while you decide

If you have a discontinued tile roof and you have lost tiles, two things matter immediately.

First, do not let anyone walk that roof who does not understand tile. More clay tile is broken by careless foot traffic during inspections and repairs than by the storms that prompted them. Every tile broken by a boot is one more you have to source.

Second, save every piece. Even cracked tile is a reference for matching, and intact salvaged pieces from your own roof are the best possible replacements for it. Do not let a crew sweep them into a dumpster.

## The point

A discontinued tile is a sourcing problem, not a death sentence for the roof. The difference between a contractor who tears off a sound historic roof and one who matches the tile and saves it is almost entirely a difference in knowledge and effort, not in what is physically possible.
