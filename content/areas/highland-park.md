## Houses built for a permanent roof

Highland Park was laid out in 1907, and University Park grew up beside it through the 1920s. A lot of the houses from those years, the Tudors, the French and Georgian revivals, the Mediterranean homes, were designed around a heavy roof that was meant to stay put: slate, clay tile, and copper where the roof planes meet.

A century later, many of those roofs are coming up for a decision. Some are original and wearing out. Some were replaced along the way with something lighter and cheaper. And some are perfectly good stone that keeps leaking for reasons that have nothing to do with the stone. Our job is to tell you which one you have, and then to build a roof that suits the house.

## What we see on Park Cities roofs

**Good slate, failing metal.** Slate can outlast two or three sets of flashing. When a slate roof leaks, the cause is usually a valley, a chimney, or a wall flashing that has rusted out or was sealed instead of soldered. Often the fix is the metal, not the roof.

**Hail.** North Texas hail is the most common reason these roofs get replaced early. When it happens, we document the damage, walk the roof with the adjuster, and explain what a specialty roof really costs to put back. We never talk policy, but we do help everyone understand the roof.

**Patchwork from past repairs.** Mismatched slate, synthetic pieces mixed into a stone roof, galvanized flashing, and a lot of caulk. We see it on almost every older roof we inspect.

**Houses that lost their original roof.** Plenty of Park Cities homes built for slate or tile are wearing a composition roof today. Going back to natural slate is one of the most common projects we do here.

## The slates we install most here

Most of our Park Cities roofs are Vermont slate: Vermont Black, Unfading Gray, and blends of purple, gray and green that we lay out piece by piece so the color reads evenly from the street. We also install Spanish slate from La Baña, like the clipped Galcar on our University Park roof. Every one of them gets copper at the valleys, chimneys and walls. If your framing can't carry a full slate roof, [lightweight slate](/materials/lightweight-slate) gets you real stone at a fraction of the weight.
