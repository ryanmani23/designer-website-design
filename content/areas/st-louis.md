## A city that was built to be roofed in tile

By 1900, St. Louis was the second-largest maker of roofing tile in the country. Companies like the [Mound City Roofing Tile Company](/blog/mound-city-roofing-tile-history) fired their tile here, and builders brought in more from [Ludowici](/blog/ludowici-roof-tile-history) in Ohio. The private places of the Central West End, DeBaliviere Place and the neighborhoods around Forest Park are full of the result: clay tile and slate roofs that have now been up for more than a hundred years.

Most of those roofs were made by companies that no longer exist. When a piece breaks, you can't order another one. That is the work we like best.

## What we see on St. Louis roofs

**Freeze and thaw.** St. Louis winters cross the freezing point over and over. Water that gets into a weak tile or a soft slate freezes, expands, and breaks it from the inside. Good tile and good slate shrug it off. Cheap material and bad flashing do not.

**Storm damage.** The May 2025 tornado hit some of the city's most historic streets. We were on one of them, at Kingsbury Place, and finished the first full re-roof on the street after the storm.

**Tile that can't be bought anymore.** Mound City, Heinz and other early makers are long gone. We salvage what can be saved, set it back in the places that matter most, and match the field with tile from [Ludowici](/materials/ludowici) or [La Escandella](/materials/la-escandella).

**Old repairs.** Mismatched tile, galvanized flashing and sealant where solder belongs. They hold for a few years and then fail all at once.

## Matching a roof that's no longer made

Matching an old roof starts with knowing what it is. We identify the original tile, its profile, its maker and its color, then decide what to save and what to replace. On the Kingsbury Place job, we took off every original Mound City terminal and hip starter by hand, cut each one to seat on the new Ludowici tile, and blended two Ludowici colors so the new field looks like a century-old roof instead of a new one. If you have a discontinued tile, our [discontinued products](/discontinued) page shows the makers we match most often.
